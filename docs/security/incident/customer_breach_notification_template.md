## When to send

**Trigger:** breach affecting personal data likely to result in high risk to rights and freedoms of natural persons (§43(3)). Not every ODPC-notifiable breach triggers §43(3); §43(3) applies specifically to high-risk cases.

**High-risk indicators (any one triggers):**
- Passport numbers or DOB exposed in identifiable form (default: yes if pgcrypto-encrypted columns compromised with the encryption key)
- Payment card data or bank details exposed (rare — Paystack holds these)
- Combination of identifiers sufficient for identity fraud (name + DOB + contact + travel document)
- Sensitive category data per §44 exposed
- Attacker demonstrated intent to harm affected individuals
- Access credentials to customer accounts exposed with authentication path intact

**Not high-risk (typically):**
- Contact details alone (email + phone) if no evidence of misuse
- Booking metadata (routes flown, dates) without linked identity data
- Data protected by strong encryption at rest AND access-controlled where the encryption key was not compromised (per `docs/security/saved_travelers_encryption.md`)

**Decision authority:** Engineer + Founder + James make the §43(3) call together based on Ops-drafted risk assessment. When in doubt, notify.

## Timing

- **After ODPC filing:** notify within 7 days of ODPC filing where possible
- **Batched notifications:** notify all affected customers within one 24-hour window to avoid disparate treatment (per SOP §4.6 fairness principle)
- **Delivery channels per SOP §4.6:** (1) email from hello@tumafly.com as primary, (2) SMS via Africa's Talking, (3) WhatsApp from +254 113 165 503, (4) in-app banner if applicable
- **Do NOT delay for perfect information** — send with best-known facts, follow up with material corrections if needed

## Recipients

Affected data subjects only — never send to the broader customer base. Includes:
- Registered customers whose account data was affected
- Guest bookers reachable via bookings.contact_email
- Saved travelers whose profiles were affected (may be different persons than the account holder)

Recipient list generated by Engineer via DB query; Ops verifies count matches ODPC filing §2; both sign off before send.

## Approval chain

1. Ops drafts English version + first-pass Swahili
2. Engineer reviews English draft for factual accuracy against actual breach + technical details
3. James reviews English draft for §43(3) legal defensibility

5. Founder final review — reads exactly as customer will see
6. Founder sends via Resend from hello@tumafly.com (Reply-To: compliance@tumafly.com)

### Body

---

Dear [Customer first name],

We are writing to inform you of a security incident that affected some of your personal data held by TumaFly. We understand this news may be concerning, and we want to give you the facts, our response, and clear steps you can take to protect yourself.

**What happened**

On [DD Month YYYY], TumaFly discovered that [brief plain-language description of the incident — what was accessed, when, for how long, and how we detected it]. We contained the incident on [DD Month YYYY] at [HH:MM EAT] and immediately began investigation.

**What information was affected**

The following categories of your personal data were involved:

- [e.g. Your name, email address, and phone number]
- [e.g. Your passport number, date of birth, and nationality]
- [e.g. Your booking history: routes flown between [date range]]

The following categories were NOT affected:

- Your full payment card details (these are held by our payment processor Paystack, not by TumaFly)
- Your account password (this is stored in a form that even TumaFly cannot read)
- [Other categories confirmed not affected]

**What we have done**

Within [X hours/days] of discovery, we:

- [Concrete action, e.g. contained the incident by rotating access credentials]
- [Concrete action, e.g. patched the vulnerability that enabled the incident]
- Notified the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC) as required under Kenyan law
- Began the investigation described in this notice

We are continuing to investigate and will update you if we learn material new facts.

**What you can do**

Because your data was involved, we recommend you take the following protective steps:

1. **Be cautious of unexpected communications.** Anyone with your leaked data could attempt to contact you claiming to be from TumaFly, an airline, or a government office. Verify any unexpected message by contacting us directly using the details at the bottom of this notice.

2. **Watch for identity misuse.** If your passport data was involved, monitor for any bookings or applications you did not authorize. Report any misuse to us and to the Kenya police via [reference].

3. **Change your TumaFly password if you have an account with us.** As a precaution, even though passwords were not affected: [link to password reset].

4. **[Any incident-specific step, e.g. "If you received an M-Pesa prompt you did not initiate, do NOT approve it and contact us immediately."]**

**Your rights**

Under Kenyan law, you have the right to:

- Request a copy of the personal data we hold about you (§26 access request)
- Request correction of inaccurate data (§40 rectification)
- Request deletion of your data (§40 right to be forgotten) — please note some data must be retained for legal compliance
- Lodge a complaint with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner if you are dissatisfied with our response

To exercise any of these rights, contact compliance@tumafly.com.

**Contacting us**

We understand you may have questions or concerns. Please reach out through any of these channels:

- Email: compliance@tumafly.com
- WhatsApp: +254 113 165 503 (business hours 08:00–20:00 EAT, Mon-Sat)
- Postal: [company registered address]

Office of the Data Protection Commissioner contact for complaints:
- Website: odpc.go.ke
- Email: [current ODPC filing channel]

**Our commitment**

We are sorry that this incident occurred. Your trust matters to us, and we are committed to learning from this and strengthening our systems. Specific improvements we have committed to include:

- [Concrete improvement 1 tied to the incident]
- [Concrete improvement 2]

If you have any questions, please contact us at compliance@tumafly.com. We will respond within [X business days].

Sincerely,

[Founder name]
Founder, TumaFly Kenya
compliance@tumafly.com

## Delivery mechanics

**Primary: Email via Resend**
- From: `hello@tumafly.com` (customer-facing brand address per SOP §4.6)
- Reply-To: `compliance@tumafly.com` (routes to Ops for handling)
- Subject per section above
- HTML + plaintext versions
- Both English + Swahili versions in the SAME email (English first, Swahili below, clear divider) — respects both languages without asking customer to pick

**Secondary: SMS via Africa's Talking (critical severity or unreachable-by-email)**
- Short message: "TumaFly security notice — check your email at [email] for full details. Reply STOP to opt out of security notifications."
- Do NOT include full breach details in SMS

**Tertiary: WhatsApp from +254 113 165 503 (critical severity only)**
- Short message: "TumaFly security notice — please check your email at [email] for full details. If you cannot access email, reply to this message and we will send full details here."
- Do NOT include full breach details in WhatsApp (message length + delivery reliability + screenshot risk)

**Fallback: postal (if email + SMS + WhatsApp all fail after 48 hours)**
- Only for customers with confirmed physical addresses
- Same content as email version
- Track delivery via registered post

## Tone guidance

- **Direct, not defensive.** Say what happened. Don't hedge.
- **Human, not corporate.** "We are sorry" not "TumaFly regrets the inconvenience."
- **Specific, not vague.** "Your passport number was accessed" not "some data was exposed."
- **Actionable.** Give concrete steps the customer can take today.
- **Non-alarmist but honest.** Don't downplay real risk. Don't manufacture urgency.
- **Cultural sensitivity.** Kenyan customers respond differently to formal English vs. Kenyan English. Swahili version is not a translation — it's a parallel, culturally-tuned version.
